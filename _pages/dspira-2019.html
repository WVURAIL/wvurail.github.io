01

A different kind of summer

By 2019 the shape of the program was settled: four weeks in Morgantown building a horn antenna, a low-noise amplifier and a software spectrometer, then two weeks at the Green Bank Observatory observing with it.

What was different was who turned up. Four of the six teachers had already been through it, two of them for the third time — which meant the summer did not have to start from first principles. The returning teachers already knew GNU Radio well enough to write their own blocks, and had spent the intervening school years running these projects with their own students.

So the emphasis moved. Less time on getting the instrument working; more on consolidating three years of labs, lectures and classroom activities into something another teacher could pick up cold.

02

Written by the teachers

The clearest output of the final summer was a site the teachers curated themselves. Classroom Radio Astronomy collected the lessons and exercises they had developed and tested — written by the people who had actually run them in front of students, rather than by the faculty who ran the program.

That material has since been folded into the main lessons site, which is where new visitors should start. The original is kept online because it is linked from elsewhere.

03

What outlasted it

2019 was the third and final cohort. Fifteen teachers from eight states had filled twenty-one places across the three summers, and what they built together is still standing: {{ site.data.education.materials.lesson_count }} free lessons across nine modules, covering the astronomy, the hardware, the signal processing and the labs.

The program's own conference paper came out the same year — and two of its four authors, John Makous and Howard Chun, are teachers from these cohorts rather than staff. That was the point of the whole exercise.
