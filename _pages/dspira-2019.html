01

A different kind of summer

The 2019 program followed an established structure. Teachers spent four weeks in Morgantown building a horn, low-noise amplifier, and software spectrometer. They then observed for two weeks at Green Bank Observatory.

What was different was who turned up. Four of the six teachers had participated before, including two attending for the third time. The summer could build on their existing knowledge. Returning teachers could already write GNU Radio blocks. During the intervening school years, they had run these projects with their students.

So the emphasis moved. They spent less time making the instruments work. Instead, they organized three years of labs, lectures, and activities for teachers new to the program.

02

Written by the teachers

The clearest output of the third in-person summer was a site the teachers curated themselves. Classroom Radio Astronomy collected lessons and exercises written by participating teachers. These authors had developed and tested the material with students.

That site was archived in 2026. All its content moved to the main lessons site alongside the summer institute's repository material. The teachers' AP Physics velocity-curve unit is there in full. The old address still resolves, but only to a page pointing here.

What came next

2019 closed the three in-person cohorts documented on this site, but it did not close DSPIRA. NSF Award {{ ed.award.number }} continued through {{ ed.award.end | date: "%Y" }}. Beginning in summer 2020, participants shifted their effort into the public lessons site and remote collaboration.

Teachers kept building after the original award ended, updating lesson material in 2025 while community contributors helped telescope users. A follow-on workshop brought DSPIRA to another group of educators. The collection now includes {{ ed.materials.lesson_count }} free lessons across {{ ed.materials.modules | size }} modules.

The program published its conference paper in 2019. Two of its four authors, John Makous and Howard Chun, were cohort teachers. That was the point of the whole exercise.
